## Problem Statement

Causal Lab needs a promo page on GitHub Pages (Advanced Programming, Session 8, Homework 9, due Saturday 14 November 2026, 20:00). The page must say what the program is for and who it is for, link to the running app, and later carry a 30-second video (Homework 10, 21 November) and a link to five slides (28 November). Three readers: a classmate, the teacher, and a stranger who has never heard of it.

## What Makes This Cool

The "whoa" the author chose (Q1): **the question first**. You type a question; Causal Lab refuses to pick a method until it knows what you are asking. "Is smoking associated with lung cancer?" comes back as unclear, with *smoking* and *lung cancer* marked "to confirm", and no method. Most method-choice tools (decision trees such as MethodAtlas or Valli's panel-data tree) start from a design the student already knows ("do you have a threshold?"); Causal Lab starts from the sentence the student wrote (Q2: the alternative students use today is asking a chatbot, which names a method).

The page then shows why that care matters: on a simulated world with a known effect, DiD can look fine and be badly wrong.

## Recommended Approach

Approach B. Wireframe: none (the look is Impeccable's step, D8). The content, in order:

**1. Header (first screen)**
- Sentence, three parts (one per beat): **"Causal Lab reads your research question first, picks a method only once you confirm, and shows you where your design breaks."**
- For whom: "For students and researchers with a causal question who are about to choose a method."
- Button: **Try it** → https://causal-lab-a-virtual-laboratory-for-causal-research-busavtyebb.streamlit.app (note under it: "Free hosting: if the app is asleep, wake it and wait about half a minute.")
- Secondary link: source code on GitHub.

**2. Beat 1: It reads your question first** (screenshot: Research Question screen, real run)
"Type *Is smoking associated with lung cancer?* Causal Lab says the question is unclear, marks *smoking* and *lung cancer* 'to confirm', and asks whether you want a causal effect. It does not pick a method." (Source: README §1.)

**3. Beat 2: It picks a method only once you confirm** (screenshot: Method Recommendation screen, real run)
"Ask *What is the causal effect of receiving a scholarship on university enrollment among students whose entrance exam scores are near the scholarship eligibility cutoff?* It reads a causal effect, quotes the treatment and the outcome, and labels the cutoff as an inference, not a fact. Once you confirm the form, it recommends regression discontinuity and says why difference-in-differences would estimate something else." (Source: README §1.)

**4. Beat 3: It shows you where your design breaks** (screenshot: Guided Demo screen, real run) — the contribution
"Difference-in-differences on 1,000 simulated worlds with a known effect of −0.50. With low diverging pre-trends, the estimate is off, on average, by 47% of the true effect (bias +0.236), and the 95% interval covers the truth in only 31.3% of worlds. The usual pre-trend test warns in just 14.2% of them. This is a known problem (Roth, 2022); Causal Lab lets you watch it happen." The section carries a visible label: **"The main result of this project."** (Source: `docs/DEMO_DETAILS.md`, `causal_lab/data/demo_study/results.json`.)

**5. How it was checked**
- The full test suite runs on every push and pull request, with the newest and the minimum supported dependency versions (`.github/workflows/`). No test count is printed: it changes as tests are added.
- The simulation study: 4 threats × 5 levels × 1,000 worlds, every replication stored with its seed, judged by a rule of at most 10% bias and at least 90% coverage. The rule was fixed on 2026-10-30, after the pre-trends results were seen and before the three other threats were simulated; the page says so (source: `docs/DEMO_DETAILS.md`).
- The question reader: four frozen evaluations on 230 published article titles, failures included (`docs/evaluation/`). It never recommended a method from the question alone. Its required numbers (AGENTS.md), with links to the reports for the rest: precision 0.95 and recall 0.60 on the second set; 1.00 and 0.83 on the third, where version 2 scores 0.80; 0.96 and 0.86 on the fourth, where version 2 scores 0.79; the recall bar is 0.70, failed on the first two sets (0.64, 0.60). The page says that most of the later gain is the set, not the code.

**6. What it is not**
"Causal Lab helps you choose and understand a design. It does not establish causal identification: that rests on assumptions about the world that data can sometimes refute and never prove."

**7. Video** — slot for the 30-second video (Homework 10). Until 21 November the section is left out of the page entirely; it is added with the video.
